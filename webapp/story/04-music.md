# Step 3 — Music from Geometry

The sequence of curve crossings produced by the geodesic is converted into music. Each crossing event becomes a note; the geometric data determines both *which* note and *when* it plays.

## Mapping

- **Pitch**: each of the three cutting curves is assigned a note:
  - Curve 1 → **A** (440 Hz)
  - Curve 2 → **C♯** (554.37 Hz)
  - Curve 3 → **E** (659.25 Hz)

  Together these form an **A major triad**.

- **Rhythm**: the time interval between consecutive notes is proportional to the **hyperbolic distance** the geodesic travels between consecutive curve crossings. Short hops produce rapid sequences; long stretches between crossings produce pauses.

## Audio controls

- **Speed multiplier**: scales the playback speed. At $1\times$, one unit of hyperbolic distance maps to a fixed time interval. Higher values compress the music.
- **Max duration**: caps the total playback length in seconds.

## What to listen for

- **Regularity vs. irregularity**: surfaces with high symmetry (equal curve lengths, zero twists) tend to produce more regular rhythmic patterns.
- **Relative frequency of notes**: on a separating surface, curve 1 (the separating curve) is crossed more often than curves 2 and 3, producing a dominant A note. On a nonseparating surface, the three curves may be crossed more evenly.
- **Clustering**: bursts of rapid notes correspond to the geodesic running nearly parallel to a cutting curve, crossing it repeatedly in quick succession.

**Try it**: run a geodesic, then press Play. Experiment with different speed multipliers to find a comfortable listening tempo.
