While for a given marimba there might be non-isometric marimbas which are isomelodic to it, they are rare.

<b>Theorem 1.2.</b>
<i> For any hyperbolic marimba $(X,\Gamma)$ there are finitely many hyperbolic marimbas $(X_1,\Gamma_1),\dots,(X_k,\Gamma_k)$ such that any other hyperbolic marimba $(X',\Gamma')$ with $\chi(X)=\chi(X')$ which is isomelodic to $(X,\Gamma)$ is isometric to one of the $(X_i,\Gamma_i)$'s. </i>

Indeed, generic marimbas are uniquely determined by their melodies.

<b>Theorem 1.3.</b> <i>If the underlying surface of an orientable hyperbolic marimba $(X,\Gamma)$ is generic, then any other orientable marimba $(X',\Gamma')$ with $\chi(X)=\chi(X')$ which is isomelodic to $(X,\Gamma)$  is isometric to $(X,\Gamma)$.</i>

To prove these theorems we exploit the analytic structure of Teichm\"uller space, adapting the argument Wolpert used to prove that generacilly the unmarked length spectrum determines the surface up to isometry. In our setting, the length spectrum is replaced by what we call the <i>$k$-orthospectrum</i>. For $k=1$, this is nothing other than the usual orthospectrum of $X\setminus\Gamma$.
